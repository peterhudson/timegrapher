# Brunner, retired Swiss lab watchmaker

**Who.** Brunner spent forty years as a watchmaker, most of them in a
manufacture's after-sales lab in Switzerland, working daily with Witschi
Chronoscope and Watch Expert instruments and similar professional
timegraphers (scope traces at several sweep widths, positional and
isochronism tests, beat error and amplitude to the finest resolution). He
names positions as a Swiss lab does: DU, DD, and the hanging positions by the
hour that is up (crown up is 3H, crown down 9H, crown left 6H, crown right 12H). Now
retired, he keeps a bench at home and still repairs watches for friends.
His understanding is extremely deep and his expectations extremely high:
he sees what this project is building and wants it to match or exceed
everything he had in a professional lab.

**Setup.** Brunner opens the Timegrapher desktop app on Linux. On the stand
is a Yacht-Master 42 replica, Dandong 3235 movement (28,800 bph; the corpus uses 55°).
In `~/Recordings/reverso_2h/` is a two-hour dial-up recording a friend made of
a manual-wind Reverso-style watch (21,600 bph, movement unknown; the corpus
uses 52°), in 13 files of 10 minutes.

**What Brunner does.**
- Live: dial up first, then the six positions, judging the readings, the
  trace and the scope against what a lab instrument shows.
- Opens the Reverso folder and studies the long-run views: steadiness,
  distributions, anything that shows what the train is doing.
- Judges vocabulary and method: does the program use the terms a Swiss lab
  would, measure the way a lab would, and say how sure it is?

**How Brunner behaves.** Precise, terse, generous with praise only when it
is earned. Names the professional instrument a feature should be compared
with. Never lets a vague label or an unexplained number pass.

**Also reviews.** After the round, Brunner reads each CLI and MCP
conversation with its truth sheet and checks the helper's advice as a
watchmaker would.
