This is a test build for a small group of testers. It is not a public release.

Download the file for your computer below. The steps for opening it the first time are in [docs/install.md](https://github.com/peterhudson/timegrapher/blob/main/docs/install.md). The builds are unsigned, so macOS and Windows will warn you once.

## What's new since 0.1.0

### Positions Test
- **Positions Test…**, beside the microphone buttons, takes you through all six positions in Witschi's order: Dial Up, Dial Down, Crown Down, Crown Left, Crown Up, Crown Right.
- Each position gets 20 seconds to settle and 60 seconds of measuring, with a progress bar. The app tells you which way to turn the watch next. Press **Next** when it is in place, or **Measure Again** after a knock.
- When the six are done you get a table of rate, amplitude and beat error for each position, with any reading outside your chosen tolerances (Men's, Ladies' or COSC) in red. Below it are the mean rate, the largest difference between positions, and vertical against horizontal for rate and amplitude, followed by the findings with Witschi's advice for each.

### Moving the watch during a take
- A ticker above the strip names the position the watch is in, so you can always see which stretch you are looking at.
- After every move the first 10 seconds are marked **Settling** and left out of the readings, while the balance recovers. They stay in the recording.
- Pausing and resuming keeps the take whole. Saving, moving the watch and opening the take again later all keep it in one piece.
- **Save Again** copies only what changed since the last save, so it is quicker on a long take.

### Readings you can trust at a glance
- Each reading now shows its confidence (High, Medium or Low) as three grey dots. Hover over the dots to see why.
- Rate, amplitude and beat error show the whole take next to the current window. Stopping keeps the readings on screen.
- An amplitude above 330° is shown as something to check rather than as a fault.
- A beat error that drifts during the recording is now flagged.
- The live screen shows no readings when no watch is on the stand, and keeps reading a watch so loud that it clips, with the clipping warning.
- A positions test run without a known lift angle says so, and grades its amplitude one step lower.
- The command line tool's quick reading and its multi-position report now find the same beats and agree with each other, and each reading lists what it is unsure of: no watch heard, beats missing, a set beat rate that disagrees with what was heard, clipping, a very short stretch, or an unknown lift angle.

### Steadiness and Scope
- New Steadiness views in the app show how steady the rate, amplitude and beat error are over the take.
- The Scope is faster and has a steady ring view.
- An Isochronism table compares the first half hour with a later one.

### Fixes from the first round of testing
- Fixes for an empty stand (no watch on the microphone), for a beat rate set by hand, and for the clock the rate is measured against.
- A saved take opens from its own folder, or from the folder it was saved into.
- Many wording, sidebar and hover help changes from the first round of Mac testing.

### For AI assistants
- The downloads include the MCP server, so Claude and other assistants can run a test and show the app's charts in the chat, with the same colours. On macOS and Windows a Claude Desktop extension (the `.mcpb` file) installs it in one step.
- New in the chat: a Live view while a position records, Steadiness and Distribution panes, and a built-in "Test My Watch" prompt.
- The watchmaker guide the assistant reads now gives a source for each tolerance and has more guidance for first-time users.
- The assistant's guide now checks what limits each reading before quoting it, and tells you when a reading shouldn't be trusted.
